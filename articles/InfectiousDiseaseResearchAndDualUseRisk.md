Concern: research meant to protect the public from dangerous pathogens can also produce knowledge, materials, or methods that could be misused for bioterrorism.

After the 2001 anthrax attacks, the United States increased the funding for research on dangerous infectious agents. These “select agents” include pathogens such as anthrax, Ebola, Marburg, yellow fever, avian influenza, pox viruses, Japanese encephalitis virus, and botulinum neurotoxins. The factual premise is that these agents are dangerous whether they arise naturally, accidentally, or through deliberate release.

Dilema: Scientists need to study live or inactive agents, infected animals, and genetic data to create vaccines and treatments. However, the same research can accidentally or intentionally generate more dangerous knowledge or organisms, such as more virulent strains, drug-resistant strains, or even virulent forms of previously harmless organisms.

Should scientists be morally responsible for harmful uses of research they did not intend? -> comparison with the moral debates surrounding the Manhattan Project. Even if researchers have good intentions, they still have a responsibility to anticipate possible misuse and take precautions.
