Computer vision is the act of interpreting and extracting meaning from images or video, mimicing human vision with a computer.

Computer vision has applications ranging from spotting cancer cells in medical images to mapping land use in satellite photos. It’s use for identifying human and human’s ‘suspicious’ behaviour is increasing. Some moral concerns related to this technology are:

- AI-powered systems can be prone to error, including biases introduced by human users, meaning that the people already most disempowered in society are also the most likely to be disadvantaged by surveillance.
- Science that can help to catch criminals, secure national borders and prevent theft can also be used to monitor political groups, suppress protest and identify battlefield targets. In many countries, legislation around surveillance technologies is patchy, and the conditions surrounding consent often lie in a legal grey area.
- Training data can violate privacy. AI training data concerning real people were found to have been created without their consent.
